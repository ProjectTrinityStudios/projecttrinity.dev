October 2026: Asha takes up her guns, the pre-alpha, and a choice to rebuild

Covers October 2026. Written in November 2026 from the project's records.

October opened with the biggest change any hero has had. On October 2nd Asha stopped throwing bottles all the time and became a gunner: a pistol in each hand, and a chain of shots that ends with both guns firing at once. Her potions now set zones on the ground, and her guns pop them.

A week later came a milestone. On October 9th the first pre-alpha came together: Patchwork's Hunt, with a brand-new tutorial called First Watch as an optional first chapter. On the ROG Xbox Ally X it averaged 90 frames a second and never dropped below 60, and it was given the go.

Then the party went somewhere new. On October 10th the first dungeons were reshaped: Patchwork's Hunt, and a second, darker place called the Tear, which the menu names the Hungry Deep. Through the rest of the month the Tear filled up:

  • The Giant, whose Storm gives you a two-and-a-half-second warning before three heavy hits.
  • Old Hunger, the Glutton, in a court of his own. He breathed for the first time on October 19th, and by October 23rd he could sweep with his tail, retch, burrow underground, leave pools of bile and glow as he fills up.
  • The Lamp Maera, the lighthouse keeper from July, now possessed. Her new body was accepted on October 24th. That night her dark look was set and a beast form of the Warden joined her, and her first voice lines were picked the next day.
  • Packs of Imps and Brutes, and a captive to find in a side room.

The party got smarter as the dungeon got harder. The tank now taunts back a boss that wanders off him, Imps go after whoever healed last, and a fight can tell the tank where to stand.

Her new kit settled in. On October 3rd her Glow Up zone, which speeds up and sharpens any friend standing in it, was set to 6 metres, and her hands learned to close properly around her pistols.

And on October 21st the project made the biggest call of the year. The game could do everything it was meant to, but a fresh-eyes review of how it was built, told to assume everything was bad, found plenty to do differently. The game had proved that it works and that it can do everything. Now it had to be done right. So the project chose to rebuild the game, one for one, in a fresh Unity project, starting with the Tear, keeping the rules of every fight exactly as they were. It was called Path B.
