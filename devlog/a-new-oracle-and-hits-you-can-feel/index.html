Devlog · November 28th, 2026

A new build: a new Oracle, and hits you can feel

[GAP: first still from the build, the new Oracle]

A new build of Project Trinity: Shatterbound is out for playtesters [GAP: version]. Here's what it adds since the build of November 24th, and what comes next.

[GAP: short clip from the build]

Hits you can feel

A hit now lands where the blade or the shot actually meets the body, with its blood or sparks right there. Heavier hits hold the fight for a beat longer, and the bodies carry on smoothly after it. Shots kick as they leave the gun. Heroes and mobs lean away from a blow, and the bosses shudder under the party's hits. Bastion, Reaver and Asha have new hit sounds.

A new Oracle

The Oracle is an astrologer now: a layered robe with pointed hems, a halo, and a braid that swings as she moves. Her Flicker Step blinks the full distance the way she faces.

[GAP: still of the Oracle]

A sharper party

Bastion pulls a boss cleanly now, without walking it off, and his dodge waits for the skill he is casting. Under Focus Adds, the healer and the damage dealers share one target and hold it until it falls. The General has two new commands: Stack on Me and Spread on Me.

Behind it is the gauntlet, a growing set of small test fights the party runs through, each checking one thing it has to get right, like reaching safety across the boss or breaking a hold on an ally.

Maera, harder

The Lamp Maera's hardest tier is a real fight. She has twice the health, walks to the middle at half health to raise her Riptide Veil, and rests between her big moves. Her crawlers sit on the vents and clog them until they're cleared. In the deep she takes her Warden form, a body of living water.

[GAP: Maera's two fixes, once they land]

Graphics and settings

DLSS and FSR 2 upscaling are in, on the cards that support them, along with anti-aliasing and shadow settings that change live. The first launch opens a short setup for the screen, brightness, subtitles and text size. A performance overlay can be turned on in Settings.

The loading screen has a little target practice to play while the dungeon loads, with cards about the worlds, and the fight starts when you press to begin.

Effects

Asha's Glow Up is a dance floor: she snaps a glowstick, and the floor lights up in pink and lime tiles. A hero who can't be hit, mid-dodge or arriving from a swap, now leaves an afterimage in their own colour.

What comes next

More refining before new content, the AI first, so the party moves smoothly and reads like real players. Playtesters will get a simple way to say how a build went, right here on the site. Don Fortuna, the next boss, waits until then.
